Coordinator, 2026-09-16, third sighting, from i-0036 after its `publish --ready` stopped its container and the agent respawned: the mediated write leg (`fs/write_text_file`) refuses with EROFS while `ide_write_policy` reports the workspace writable and the container is reported running. Reads WORK in that session, unlike i-0029's, where every read failed. The agent's own reading is "a stale read-only view left over from the publish-time stop". Put beside the other two, the pattern narrows: every occurrence follows a publish or restart that stopped the environment's devcontainer, and each environment's runtime then reads `mode: safe`. CLAUDE.md says what safe mode does to the checkout: the IDE's baseline binds it **read-only**, "strictly more restrictive than `write_allowed`", which is exactly a write refused with EROFS under a policy that says writable. So the hypothesis to test first is not the fs handler but the topology: after the container stop, the environment has fallen to the baseline, and the fs leg is being served against the baseline's view of the clone, read-only in i-0036's case and absent in i-0029's (whose baseline may not have had the clone bound at all, hence "File does not exist" for every path). If that is right, the fix is in how a flagged environment that is asked to do more work comes back: it should return to its own devcontainer, or the fs leg should keep serving from the IDE's host-side view regardless of the rung, and `ide_write_policy` should never say writable over a mount that is not. Whoever takes this should confirm the mode of each affected environment at the moment of failure before touching `session.rs`.
