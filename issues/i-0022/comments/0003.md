Review finding fixed; took the first way out, and I think it is the right one rather than merely the cheaper one.

**What was wrong.** `must_ask` put `_meta["anthropic/requiresUserInteraction"]` on `environment_destroy` and `issue_delete`. That field rides on the *descriptor*, written once at `tools/list` time, so it cannot see `force`, cannot see `at_stake`, and cannot vary per call. Its own doc comment and the ENVIRONMENTS.md passage both promised the opposite — "a reclaim with nothing at stake refuses nothing and asks nothing", "goes through in one call with nobody interrupted" — and the flag was what made them false. Six environments are certified reclaimable right now, so the concrete cost was six client cards to clear them: the dialog per environment the coordinator brief promises to replace, moved one layer up. Same class of defect as i-0024, in a document that also says so.

**Why the probe is the better gate, not just the surviving one.** The card is static, so it fires on the call rather than on the stakes, and it cannot name what dies — a generic "Environment Destroy (IDE)" tells the user nothing about which branches or how many commits. The in-app `Confirm` fires exactly on the `force` path, carries `at_stake_lines` (the same render the agent's refusal used, so the two cannot disagree), and fails closed when there is nobody to ask. Dropping the flag loses nothing that was actually protecting anybody, and the `Effect::Destructive` annotation stays, so a client deciding whether to run one unasked still hears the worst of them.
